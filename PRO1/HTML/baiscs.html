Quick Grammar Overview

Parts of Speech

Words in English are grouped as nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, prepositions, conjunctions, and interjections.

Tenses

Show time of an action: present, past, and future forms help express when something happens.

Sentence Structure

Every sentence usually has a subject, a verb, and a complete idea.

Punctuation

Correct punctuation improves clarity, meaning, and flow in writing.

1. Parts of Speech

  • Noun: names a person, place, thing, or idea. Example: book, London, happiness.
  • Pronoun: replaces a noun. Example: he, she, it, they.
  • Verb: shows action or state. Example: run, is, write, think.
  • Adjective: describes a noun. Example: beautiful, bright, old.
  • Adverb: describes a verb, adjective, or another adverb. Example: quickly, very, happily.
  • Preposition: shows relation between words. Example: in, on, at, under.
  • Conjunction: joins words or sentences. Example: and, but, because.

2. Tenses

English verbs change according to time.

Present Simple I play football every day.
Past Simple She visited her grandmother yesterday.
Future Simple We will travel next week.
Tip: Use the correct tense to show when an action happens.

3. Sentence Structure

  • A sentence must have a subject and a verb.
  • It should express a complete thought.
  • Examples:
    • She reads books.
    • The children are playing outside.

Simple sentence: Birds fly.

Compound sentence: I studied hard, and I passed the exam.

Complex sentence: Because it was raining, we stayed home.

4. Common Grammar Errors

  • Subject-verb agreement: He goes not He go.
  • Wrong pronoun usage: It is I instead of It is me in formal speech.
  • Misplaced articles: a book, an apple, the sun.
  • Punctuation mistakes: use commas, full stops, and apostrophes correctly.
